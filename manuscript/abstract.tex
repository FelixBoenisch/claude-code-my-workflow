\noindent Experimental evidence suggests that delegation can shift responsibility for adverse outcomes onto human intermediaries. We ask whether the same motive applies to delegation to algorithms. %We ask whether responsibility can likewise be shifted onto algorithms and whether the prospect of such a shift, in turn, motivates delegation.
In a preregistered online experiment with $322$ participants, a decision-maker chooses to make a payoff-relevant prediction for a matched recipient or delegate it to an algorithm, calibrated to perform equally well. We vary between subjects whether the recipient can impose punishment. The prospect of punishment reduces delegation by $18.3$ percentage points, from $60.8\%$ to $42.5\%$, reversing the direction that the responsibility-shifting account predicts. Delegation, in turn, offers no protection. Recipients condition punishment primarily on the realized outcome, not on whether the prediction was delegated. %Delegation offers no protection in return. Recipients punish delegated and non-delegated predictions alike and condition punishment on the realized outcome. 
We discuss several candidate mechanisms for this reversal.
