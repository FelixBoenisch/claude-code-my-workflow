# Burton, Stein & Jensen (2020) — *A systematic review of algorithm aversion in augmented decision making*

## Type

**Systematic literature review.** Synthesises 61 peer-reviewed articles on algorithm aversion published between 1950 and 2018.

## Research question

Why do people misuse (under- or over-utilize) algorithmically generated insights in augmented decision-making? The paper traces the algorithm-aversion concept across disciplines and organises proposed causes / solutions into themes.

## Methods

- Systematic literature search across 8 databases (JSTOR, Wiley, ScienceDirect, Taylor & Francis, SAGE, ACM, IEEE, EBSCOhost).
- 61 articles meeting inclusion criteria.
- Thematic synthesis.

## Five thematic groups of explanations for algorithm aversion

1. **Expectations and expertise**: people expect algorithms to be perfect and react harshly when expectations are violated.
2. **Decision autonomy**: people want to retain control over their decisions; algorithms remove that.
3. **Incentivization**: incentive structures may not align user preferences with algorithm use.
4. **Cognitive compatibility**: algorithms' decision processes don't match how humans reason.
5. **Divergent rationalities**: algorithms and humans optimise different objectives.

## Main conclusions

1. **Algorithm aversion is multi-causal** — no single explanation.
2. **Themes interact** — e.g., expectations and decision autonomy reinforce each other.
3. **Theory integration is needed** — current explanations are fragmented.
4. **Empirical research agenda**: the paper proposes specific questions for future behavioural-decision-making research.
