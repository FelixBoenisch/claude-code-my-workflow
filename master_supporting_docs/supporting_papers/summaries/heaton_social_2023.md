# Heaton, Clos, Nichele & Fischer (2023) — *The Social Impact of Decision-Making Algorithms*

## Type

**Literature review** at the TAS '23 ACM symposium. Conceptual / synthesis paper, not an empirical study.

## Research question

How do the **perceived agency, responsibility, and accountability** of decision-making algorithms influence public **trust** and **blame**? The paper synthesizes existing scholarship (2000–2023) on these concepts.

## Methods

Systematic literature review. Inclusion/exclusion criteria:
- Published 2000–2023.
- Related to responsible research and innovation (RRI), ethics, governance, public engagement.
- Focused on decision-making algorithms (excluding broader AI/ML literature without decision-making focus).

Synthesises findings using the AREA 4P (Anticipate, Reflect, Engage, Act) responsible research and innovation framework.

## Main findings

1. **Public trust in algorithms is shaped by perceived agency** — both human agency (in the design and oversight loop) and algorithmic agency (the system's perceived autonomy).
2. **Responsibility and accountability are conceptually distinct**: responsibility concerns ethical design; accountability concerns answerability for outcomes.
3. **Blame allocation in algorithmic systems is contested**: should it be shared among developers, operators, regulators, and the algorithms themselves?
4. **Gaps in the empirical literature**: limited examination of how trust and blame are *impacted* by perceived social agency, responsibility, and accountability of systems.
5. **Need for more empirical research** on the relationship between these concepts.
