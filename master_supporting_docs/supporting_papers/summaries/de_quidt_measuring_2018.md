# de Quidt, Haushofer & Roth (2018) — *Measuring and Bounding Experimenter Demand*

## Type

**Methodological paper** introducing a technique for measuring and bounding experimenter demand effects in lab and survey experiments.

## Research question

Experimenter demand effects (participants modifying their behaviour based on what they think the researcher wants to find) are a long-standing concern in experimental economics. **How can we bound the magnitude of these effects?**

## Methods

- **Demand treatments**: deliberately tell some subjects what the researcher's preferred result is (e.g., "we expect to find more cooperation in the treated condition").
- The treated subjects' response provides an *upper bound* on the demand effect; the control gives the "natural" behaviour.
- The gap between the two estimates the maximum possible demand bias.
- Applied to 11 classic experimental tasks (dictator game, public goods, risk preferences, etc.).

## Key results

- **Demand effect bounds average 0.13 standard deviations** across tasks.
- **Demand effects are modest** — most classic experimental results are robust to demand effects of this size.
- **The method allows demand-robust estimation** of treatment effects.

## Main takeaways

1. **Experimenter demand is real but modest** in most lab settings.
2. **Bounds can be computed** using the demand-treatment technique.
3. **Most existing experimental literature is robust** to plausible demand effects.
