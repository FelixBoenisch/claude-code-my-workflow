# Fiorina (1986) — *Legislator Uncertainty, Legislative Control, and the Delegation of Legislative Power*

## Research question

Why do legislatures delegate authority to administrative agencies rather than legislating directly? Standard public-choice answers (expertise, complexity) are incomplete. Fiorina proposes that **legislators delegate to disguise their responsibility** for policy outcomes — particularly when constituents' *perceived* benefits of regulation may differ from *actual* benefits.

## Methods

Historical / public-choice essay. The motivating case study is the founding of the Interstate Commerce Commission (1887) — Fiorina uses the legislative history of the Reagan vs Cullom bills to illustrate how the disagreement was less about the substance of regulation than about *the mechanism of enforcement* (direct court enforcement vs administrative agency). The choice of mechanism, he argues, is itself a political-strategic choice about who bears blame for outcomes.

## Key arguments

1. **Legislators face uncertainty** about constituents' valuation of policy outcomes.
2. **Direct legislation exposes legislators to blame** when outcomes disappoint.
3. **Delegation to administrative agencies disguises responsibility**: voters can't easily attribute outcomes to specific legislative choices.
4. **The agency becomes a "lightning rod"** for blame.
5. The model extends beyond regulation to other forms of delegation: international agencies, blue-ribbon commissions, formula rules.

## Main takeaways

1. **Legislative delegation has a *blame-shifting* function** beyond efficiency.
2. **The mechanism is information asymmetry** between voters and legislators.
3. **Public-choice analysis must include* the political incentives to delegate, not just the efficiency rationale.
4. **Foundational citation** for the responsibility-shifting tradition in political economy.

**Why this paper matters for the manuscript:**

**(1) Foundational citation for the blame-shifting tradition.** Like Weaver, Fiorina is the political-science antecedent of the experimental literature. The manuscript's H1 — that punishment possibility motivates delegation — is the lab version of Fiorina's claim about legislators.

**(2) The "information asymmetry" mechanism in Fiorina is *closed off* by the manuscript's design.** Fiorina's mechanism depends on voters not being able to attribute outcomes to specific legislative choices. The manuscript's design makes the algorithm transparent (Bernoulli at known probability) — Player B can attribute outcomes precisely. So Fiorina's specific channel doesn't apply, but the *general* framework (delegation as strategic blame-shifting) does.

**(3) Useful for the manuscript's introduction.** Fiorina provides a high-status, foundational citation for the importance of the responsibility-shifting question. The manuscript could quote the canonical Fiorina passage on the ICC as a real-world example of strategic delegation for blame-disguising purposes.
