# Holt & Laury (2002) — *Risk Aversion and Incentive Effects*

## Type

**Methodological / measurement paper** introducing what has become the standard **Holt-Laury multiple price list (MPL)** for eliciting risk preferences.

## Research question

How can risk aversion be cleanly measured, and how does it change with incentive size? The paper introduces a paired-lottery-choice menu that produces a single switching point — the row at which subjects switch from the safe option to the risky one — that can be mapped to a CRRA risk-aversion coefficient.

## Methods

- **Ten paired lottery choices** between Option A (safer, narrower range) and Option B (riskier, wider range).
- Each row has the same probabilities, increasing across the table from 10% high-payoff in row 1 to 100% in row 10.
- A risk-neutral subject switches at row 5; risk averse subjects switch later; risk-seeking earlier.
- Three payoff scales: low (e.g., max $3.85), 20× scale, 50× scale.

## Key results

- Most subjects are risk averse at modest stakes, more so at higher stakes — risk aversion increases with stake size.
- The MPL produces a clean switching point and reliable risk-aversion estimates.
- The method has become the **standard** for risk-preference elicitation in experimental economics.

**Why this paper matters for the manuscript:**

**(1) Methodological tool.** The manuscript uses the Holt-Laury MPL to elicit Player A's risk preferences. This is the standard implementation; the citation is appropriate.

**(2) Possible moderator.** Risk aversion may moderate the H1 effect — more risk-averse subjects might delegate more (to avoid the risk of bad performance) or less (to avoid the risk of being seen as not engaging). The manuscript doesn't currently report a risk-aversion-by-treatment interaction; this could be a useful robustness check.

**(3) Standard citation, no controversy.** Holt-Laury is one of the most cited methodological references in experimental economics. Use as-is.
