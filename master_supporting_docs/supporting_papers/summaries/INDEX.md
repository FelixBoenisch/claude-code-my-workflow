## Cross-cutting observations from the summaries

A few patterns worth flagging that emerged across the 57 summaries:

1. **Vignette-vs-lab gap.** Vignette studies (Bigman, Shank, Tobia, Hill, Pezzo, Naquin & Kurtzberg, Tsumura) consistently find strong moral-attribution asymmetries between humans and machines. Incentivised lab studies (Kirchkamp, Feier, Chevrier, Maasland, **this manuscript**) find weaker and more variable effects. The H2 null in your manuscript is consistent with the lab-study pattern.

2. **Task-type matters enormously.** Dictator games with explicit fair/unfair choices (B&F, Hamman, Coffman, Oexl, Chevrier, Tontrup) yield strong responsibility-shifting effects. Prediction tasks with aligned incentives (Kirchkamp; **this manuscript**) yield weaker or null effects. This is the strongest moderator pattern in the literature.

3. **The "preference for autonomy" framing is converging.** Ivanova-Stenzel & Tolksdorf 2025 ("preference for decision autonomy"), Bénabou & Tirole 2011 (identity as asset), Dietvorst 2018 (modifiability), Jauernig 2022 (moral discretion), and your M3 (process ownership) all point toward similar mechanisms. The manuscript's contribution sharpens this by isolating *accountability salience* as the trigger that activates the autonomy preference.

4. **Recent (2025–2026) papers don't directly compete with the manuscript's design.** Tontrup & Sprigman 2025 is closest, but uses a dictator-game setting with strong self-interest. The manuscript's individual-level calibration + delegator/evaluator separation + punishment on/off manipulation remains distinctive.

5. **Three Tier-1 actions to consider:**
   - Use the Weitzner microfoundation (literature.tex ¶39) more explicitly.
   - Add citations to Tontrup, Jolly, Kormylo, Dargnies in literature.tex (they're in the bib but not yet `\cite`d).
   - Soften the design.tex ¶39 citation of Dietvorst 2015 (his finding is about confidence loss, not ex-ante competence).
